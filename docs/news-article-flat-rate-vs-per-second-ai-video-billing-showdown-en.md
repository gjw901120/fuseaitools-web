        <section class="crossover">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">The Crossover Arithmetic</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Variable 1 — duration.</strong> Flat prices cap your exposure: a Sora 2 Pro 15-second high render is 480 credits whether it works or not. On the meter, the same ambition (Wan 1080p × 15s) is 720, and the per-second camp's real ceiling play (30-second Wan clips at 1,440) has no flat-camp equivalent at all. Under ~8 seconds the flat camp wins nearly every matchup; over ~12, the meter's granularity starts paying for itself — because on a meter you can also <em>shrink</em> the deliverable.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Variable 2 — iterations per accepted clip.</strong> This is where the structures truly diverge. Flat: every draft costs the full price whether you use 2 seconds of it or delete it at submission review. Meter: drafts at the cheap tier are the camp's superpower — 5 seconds of Wan 480p is 60 credits, less than a single Sora Pro attempt at 120. A workflow that drafts cheap and finals once lives in the meter; a workflow that rolls full-length candidates lives in flat.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Variable 3 — variance tolerance.</strong> If the platform accepts "whatever the model decided" (ambient loops, B-roll, test cards), flat's 10–48-credit generations are the cheapest video on the shelf, period. If the client signed off on 1080p, 12 seconds, exact aspect ratio — the meter is the only camp selling that, and the price of admission is the per-second rate.</p>
        </section>

            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Goal: 20 usable 7-second social clips in a month, 1-in-3 keep rate. <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Flat invoice:</strong> 60 Sora 2 generations at 10 = 600, plus 20 Veo Lite quality spikes at 24 = 480 → <strong style="color:#818cf8;">1,080 credits list</strong> — for 20 clips you <em>select from variance</em>, at whatever length and look the models decided. <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Meter invoice for 20 clips to an exact brief</strong> (7s, 1080p, Wan at 48/sec): finals alone are 6,720, plus ~720 if you pre-viz every shot at 480p first. That's the honest framing: the camps aren't selling the same clip. A 6× price gap for locked resolution, locked duration, locked framing, and revision-to-spec is a bargain to a client with a spec — and nonsense to a meme page.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">What discipline actually changes is the overhead ratio inside the meter camp: drafting once at 480p (12/sec) instead of re-rolling 1080p finals adds ~10% to the invoice and typically halves the discarded-final count. And at Ultra monthly (63%), the flat invoice lands near ~680 credits versus the undisciplined meter's ~4,690 — a gap no plan tier closes, which is precisely why the crossover deserves a spreadsheet instead of a habit.</p>
