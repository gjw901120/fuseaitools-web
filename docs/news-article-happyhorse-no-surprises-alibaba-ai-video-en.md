            <h3>The Shot Narrative Gap: From "Near-Production" to "Breakdown Scenes"</h3>
            <p>Blind-test scores are just the entry ticket. The real differentiator for AI video models isn't "can it generate pretty frames" but "can it tell a story through shots."</p>

            <p>In multi-shot consistency stress tests, evaluators pushed the model to its limits with <strong>11 visual anchor points</strong> across three shots — 9 remained stable, with only ring count and lip color showing drift. This means HappyHorse-1.0 has crossed the most basic threshold from single-shot display to multi-shot narrative, landing at a <strong>"near-production"</strong> level overall.</p>

            <p>However, in longer narratives and complex scenes, problems emerge. In a 15-second test, a swordsman suddenly gained an extra sword in his left hand mid-swing. In crowd-heavy scenes, background faces turned blurry with noticeably weak motion. Independent testing by <strong>Zhidongxi</strong> confirmed the same: videos over 10 seconds are prone to physics bugs — frozen expressions, chairs materializing out of thin air, and other artifacts.</p>

            <p>By contrast, 5-second short clips performed passably. As one tester put it: <em>"It's v1.0 — for a 1.0 launch, this is already a solid start."</em></p>

            <h3>Audio-Visual Sync: Progress and Shortfalls</h3>
            <p>HappyHorse-1.0 showed genuine promise in audio-visual synchronization. In complex body-motion tests, instantaneous sound effects aligned accurately with jump-and-land actions at <strong>frame-level precision</strong>. In film production, this normally requires heavy investment across pre-production, shooting, and post-editing. HappyHorse's one-shot generation is a qualitative productivity leap.</p>

            <p>But for finer-grained tasks like musical instrument performance, v1.0's shortcomings became evident — the visual changes and audio rhythm were <strong>clearly out of sync</strong>. v1.1 showed only marginal improvement in this niche scenario. Lip-sync accuracy for multi-language dialogue reached <strong>98.2%</strong> in v1.0 — above the industry average, but still short of professional-use perfection.</p>
