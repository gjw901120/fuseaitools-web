        <section class="native-multimodal">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">I. Native Multimodality: Not Just "Putting DALL·E Inside ChatGPT"</h2>

            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">When GPT-4o-Image launched in March 2025, the most common misunderstanding was this: many people thought it was simply DALL·E stuffed into GPT-4o. In reality, these are <strong>two fundamentally different technical approaches</strong>.</p>

            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">DALL·E's logic is the <strong>"diffusion model"</strong> — gradually "denoising" from a field of random noise until an image emerges. GPT-4o-Image, on the other hand, is built on an <strong>autoregressive model</strong>, the same underlying approach GPT uses to process text. This means image generation is no longer an "add-on" but a native capability of the model itself. OpenAI trained the model on a joint distribution of images and text, enabling it not only to understand how the two relate but also to grasp relationships between images.</p>

            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">This architectural difference brings <strong>three key advantages</strong>:</p>

            <h3 style="color:#f3f4f6;">1. World Knowledge Powers Imagery</h3>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">GPT-4o-Image can directly draw on the model's built-in knowledge base to generate images. For example, asking it to "create a visual infographic explaining why San Francisco is so foggy" triggers the model to generate a chart based on geographic and climatic knowledge, rather than merely "painting a pretty city." Images become <strong>"communicable visual information"</strong> — not just aesthetically pleasing pictures.</p>

            <h3 style="color:#f3f4f6;">2. Contextual Understanding & Iterative Consistency</h3>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Because image generation is natively integrated into GPT-4o, users can continuously refine images through conversation. Even after multiple rounds of edits — changing hair color, adjusting composition, switching artistic styles — the character's appearance remains coherent. This solves the most frustrating problem in earlier AI image generation: the character "changing faces" with every single edit.</p>

            <h3 style="color:#f3f4f6;">3. Complex Prompt Following</h3>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">While most systems struggle to handle 5–8 objects, GPT-4o-Image can simultaneously coordinate <strong>10–20 distinct objects</strong>, precisely binding each one's attributes and relationships.</p>

        <section class="limitations">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">IV. Shadows Beneath the Spotlight: Capability Boundaries & the Evaluation Dilemma</h2>

            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">GPT-4o-Image was far from flawless. OpenAI openly acknowledged multiple limitations:</p>

            <ul style="color:#d1d5db;">
                <li><strong>Overly tight cropping on long images:</strong> When generating posters and other elongated images, cropping tended to be too aggressive</li>
                <li><strong>Face-editing consistency:</strong> When editing user-uploaded faces, consistency was sometimes difficult to maintain</li>
                <li><strong>Uncertainty in partial edits:</strong> Requests to modify specific parts did not always succeed and could inadvertently alter other areas</li>
                <li><strong>High-density concept generation:</strong> Accurately rendering 10–20+ concepts in a single image (e.g., a complete periodic table) remained challenging</li>
                <li><strong>Non-Latin scripts:</strong> Highly complex non-Latin language generation was prone to errors</li>
            </ul>

            <h3 style="color:#f3f4f6;">Controversial Third-Party Evaluation Results</h3>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">The <strong>RISEBench</strong> image editing reasoning benchmark, published by the Shanghai AI Laboratory and other institutions, showed that across 360 complex visual editing tasks requiring temporal, causal, spatial, and logical reasoning, GPT-4o-Image achieved a perfect completion rate of only <strong>28.9%</strong>.</p>

            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">But this result requires nuanced interpretation: RISEBench evaluates <strong>"reasoning-based image editing"</strong> capability, not general-purpose image generation. On <strong>GenEval</strong> — a general text-to-image benchmark — GPT-4o-Image scored an overall <strong>0.84</strong>, significantly surpassing all prior methods. On the <strong>Reason-Edit</strong> image editing benchmark, it scored <strong>0.929</strong>, outperforming the previous best method by 0.357 — a qualitative leap.</p>

            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">A joint evaluation report from the University of Hong Kong and other institutions also showed that in Chinese-language contexts, GPT-4o's image understanding capability ranks in the top tier alongside Claude, and actually surpasses Claude in visual reasoning & analysis as well as visual aesthetics & creativity dimensions.</p>

            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">The critical <strong>"capability mismatch"</strong>: What RISEBench exposed wasn't that "GPT-4o-Image can't generate images" — it was that its <strong>upper bound for understanding complex reasoning instructions</strong> has real limits. And this is precisely what <strong>GPT Image 2, with its "reasoning model," set out to address</strong>.</p>
