            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Pitfalls: Five Ways an Agency Month Bleeds Margin</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Variations by re-generation.</strong> Reframing a master costs a flat 6 as an edit; re-rolling "the same post in vertical" costs the same order of magnitude but re-rolls the product, the logo, and the brand. Agencies get billed twice for this: once in credits, once in the client email asking why the bottle looks different.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Testing concepts on the finals lane.</strong> Sixty ideas probed at 9 a render is 540 instead of 180 — a 360-credit penalty for skipping the canary pool, which is more than double the entire music budget.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Letting the draft pool run free.</strong> Drafts at 48 are cheap individually and ruinous in bulk: two per client is a process at 576; five per client is 1,440 — more than the entire finals line, spent on videos nobody posts.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Transcribing whole client webinars.</strong> STT bills every rounded-up minute; pulling twenty clips from a 60-minute recording bills 360 in one pass when 200 seconds of source would do. Cut the source before you cut the transcript.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Padding voice-over scripts past 1,000 bytes.</strong> A second block doubles the read. Thirty client scripts each tipping over the boundary is +300 credits of pure copy bloat — nearly seventy percent of the text-card line.</p>
