Eight lessons = <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">480 list credits</strong>. The budgeting habit that follows is arithmetic, not taste: at a ~150-word speaking pace, a minute of narration is ~950 bytes, so <em>every minute of script you keep is one block you don't pay</em>.             <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Pitfalls: Five Ways a Course Month Goes Over</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Composing slides on the Pro lane.</strong> At 24 a render, layout experimentation pays finals prices for throwaways. The flat-12 draft lane bills 12 at any allowed resolution — compose there, render approved specs on Pro.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Paying for model sound you will mute.</strong> The promo's audio is music plus the instructor's voice; leaving the Omni audio switch on costs 10 more per second (120 on a 12-second cut) for invented SFX the edit deletes.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Long scripts, quietly.</strong> Byte-billed narration and minute-billed captions both meter <em>length</em>, not quality: a lesson that rambles two extra minutes pays two blocks and two minutes on every read and every transcript. Cut the script; the invoice will thank you on all eight lessons.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">The 6:01 ceiling.</strong> STT rounds every partial minute up. Editing lesson audio to just under a whole minute is a real saving; letting it drift thirty seconds over is a phantom extra minute billed forever.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Re-rendering a slide to fix one caption.</strong> A regeneration re-rolls the deck's typography. A segment edit is flat 6 and changes only the flagged region — twenty-five fixes at 150 instead of 600.</p>
