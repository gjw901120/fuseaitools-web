            <h2>I. From 2.6 to 3.0: The Divergence of Two Technical Paths</h2>

            <h3>Kling 2.6 — The "Milestone" of Native Audio</h3>
            <p>Kling 2.6 is arguably the most transformative update in the Kling series to date — it achieved <strong>native synchronized audio generation</strong> for the first time. Previous Kling models were essentially "silent films," requiring users to manually add voiceovers, sound effects, and background music after video generation.</p>

            <p>Version 2.6 changed everything: it can simultaneously output visual frames, character voices, environmental sound effects, and background music in a single generation, with frame-level audio-visual alignment. In real-world testing, 2.6 supports <strong>six audio types</strong> — voice narration, multi-character dialogue, singing/rap, environmental sounds, object sound effects, and mixed audio. One tester noted that "audio-visual matching is excellent, with voice rhythm perfectly aligned with on-screen motion."</p>

            <p>However, 2.6's audio capabilities have boundaries: multi-character dialogue scenes involving three or more speakers may experience voice attribution inconsistencies; maximum generation duration is 10 seconds; and it currently only supports English and Chinese voice output.</p>

            <h3>Kling 3.0 — From "Generation" to "Direction"</h3>
            <p>If 2.6 solved the "let there be sound" problem, 3.0 tackles the question of <strong>"how to tell a story."</strong></p>

            <p>The Kling 3.0 series introduces a complete <strong>narrative control system</strong>:</p>

            <ul>
                <li><strong>Multi-Shot (Multiple Shot Storyboard):</strong> This is the most iconic feature of 3.0. Creators can define multiple shots within a single prompt — from wide shots to medium shots to close-ups — specifying each shot's duration, framing, perspective, content, and camera movement. The model automatically handles shot transitions while maintaining narrative coherence.</li>
                <li><strong>Element Library:</strong> The "Element Library" feature enables "asset-ization" of characters, objects, and scenes. Users upload 2–4 multi-angle reference images to generate a reusable "element," referenced by tags (e.g. @Element1) in subsequent generations to maintain appearance consistency. The 3.0 Omni version also supports binding exclusive voices to character elements, ensuring "audio-visual consistency" across different works.</li>
                <li><strong>Motion Brush:</strong> This feature allows users to draw motion paths directly on the source image, precisely specifying the movement direction and trajectory of specific elements. Compared to describing motion through text, this "draw-it-out" approach makes motion control more intuitive and accurate.</li>
                <li><strong>Native 4K Output:</strong> In April 2026, Kling 3.0 added native 4K output capability, supporting one-click generation of 3840×2160 resolution video — making it the first AI video model to achieve this technology.</li>
            </ul>

        <section class="real-world-benchmarks">
            <h2>III. Real-World Benchmarks: Kling Across Different Scenarios</h2>

            <p>In third-party benchmark tests, Kling 2.6 (Motion Control version) was compared against Seedance 1.8 and Veo 3 across five dimensions:</p>

            <h3>Scenario 1: Cinematic Feel</h3>
            <p>Kling 2.6 achieved the most convincing balance. Opening scenes felt visually grounded, with raindrops better spatially integrated into the environment, and character motion maintaining more natural alignment with surrounding elements. In comparison, Seedance's output had stronger emotional tone but weaker physics, while Veo 3 had more refined composition but temporal alignment issues in motion.</p>

            <h3>Scenario 2: Product Marketing</h3>
            <p>Veo 3 was most reliable at maintaining product integrity — though its material details were less realistic than Seedance, it at least didn't generate wrong product categories. Kling 2.6 achieved visually appealing first frames, but product form showed slight drift during motion.</p>

            <h3>Scenario 3: Realistic Motion</h3>
            <p>Seedance performed most balanced in motion — stable body proportions, consistent motion, and natural voiceover. Kling 2.6 had the most physically realistic motion mechanics, but background showed significant distortion during movement.</p>

            <h3>Scenario 4: Multi-Shot Consistency</h3>
            <p>All three models achieved basic shot-to-shot continuity. Kling 2.6 had the most distinctive visual style but sacrificed more on detail realism — for instance, a chef chopping directly on a plate placed on a cutting board. Veo 3 had the most balanced overall narrative.</p>

            <h3>Scenario 5: Character Consistency</h3>
            <p>Kling 2.6 maintained environments best but suffered a more severe problem — the first half was still anime style while the second half shifted to realistic rendering. This style rupture completely broke character continuity. Seedance performed most consistently in this round.</p>

            <p>The conclusion from these benchmarks: there is no "one-size-fits-all" model — different workflows require different tools.</p>

        <section class="business-logic">
            <h2>IV. Business Logic: Why Kling Must Go Solo</h2>

            <p>Beyond technology, Kling's commercialization path deserves equal attention.</p>

            <h3>Revenue Growth and Cost Pressure</h3>
            <p>According to Kuaishou's financial reports, Kling AI generated quarterly revenues of ¥150M, ¥250M, ¥300M, and ¥340M across four quarters in 2025, totaling approximately <strong>¥1.04 billion</strong> (~$143M). By January 2026, Kling AI's annualized revenue run rate (ARR) exceeded <strong>$300M (~¥2 billion)</strong>. Kuaishou CEO Cheng Yixiao stated, "We are confident in achieving more than double the revenue growth this year."</p>

            <p>But the revenue sprint comes with massive costs. Kuaishou's 2025 R&D spending grew 18.8% from ¥12.2 billion to ¥14.5 billion, primarily driven by AI. In 2026, Kuaishou's projected total capital expenditure is approximately <strong>¥26 billion (~$3.6B)</strong>, with Kling's large model being a major cost driver. As industry analyst Wang Chao notes, "AI investment is a bottomless pit — especially video-generation models that require enormous hardware compute resources."</p>

            <h3>Spin-Off Logic: Valuation Restructuring</h3>
            <p>The core driver behind Kling's spin-off is <strong>valuation restructuring</strong>. In January 2026, LLM companies like Zhipu AI and MiniMax listed on Hong Kong's stock exchange with strong market performance, commanding market caps exceeding HK$300 billion. Kuaishou's overall valuation has languished around HK$200+ billion — the market hasn't fully priced in its AI business value.</p>

            <p>If Kling AI were to independently raise funds at a $20 billion valuation, that alone would equal nearly 70% of Kuaishou's current market cap. Morgan Stanley noted that spinning off Kling would "significantly unlock Kuaishou's value," as pure-play AI companies typically command higher valuation multiples.</p>

            <h3>Competitive Pressure: First-Mover Advantage Eroding</h3>
            <p>Since 2026, the video-generation landscape has shifted dramatically. Before Chinese New Year, ByteDance's <strong>Seedance 2.0</strong> emerged as a strong competitor; in April, Alibaba's <strong>HappyHorse-1.0</strong> landed atop the Artificial Analysis leaderboard. Kling's first-mover advantage is being steadily eroded, facing a "blocked in front, pursued from behind" competitive landscape.</p>

            <p>More critically, the competitive direction is shifting: a video model's upper ceiling largely depends on its underlying language model. As Wang Chao points out, "Kling AI lacks a strong foundation model — maintaining a long-term lead through single-point breakthroughs is very challenging. In contrast, Alibaba and ByteDance are both advancing their video models on the foundation of their own language models."</p>
