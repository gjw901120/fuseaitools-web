            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Pitfalls: Five Ways a Compliant Channel Still Goes Off the Rails</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Regenerating instead of editing after review.</strong> Every fresh roll re-rolls the anatomy that was already approved. Masked edits (6 credits) keep the untouched pixels provably identical; regeneration keeps nothing.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Budgeting bilingual audio per language instead of per byte.</strong> Byte-based TTS pricing rewards Chinese and punishes verbose English scripts. Re-count the script in bytes before the first of twelve episodes, not after the first invoice.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">The STT one-minute cliff.</strong> Captions bill ceil(minutes) × 6: a 4:01 episode pays the 5-minute rate. Trim narration to whole minutes or accept paying for the padding.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Claim-shaped visuals.</strong> Photoreal "drug working" animations read as clinical evidence regardless of intent. The compliance-safe list is short: decorative motion, structural bumpers, illustrated (not photographic) anatomy.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Skipping the resolution mandatory field.</strong> On the Qwen image lanes, <code>resolution</code> is required and only 1K/2K are legal — but note the flat 12-credit pricing of the draft lane means resolution there is a <em>quality</em> decision, while on Pro it is a <em>money</em> decision. Confusing the two is how a department pays 24 for pixels it will redraw.</p>
