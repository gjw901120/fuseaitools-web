            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Pitfalls: Five Ways a Listing Month Overspends</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Re-rolling a staged photo to fix a badge.</strong> A fresh generation re-decides the furniture that was already disclosed and approved. The disclosure stamp is a flat-6 masked edit; the re-roll is a 24-credit staging pass plus a re-review of the whole room.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Styling at 2K instead of probing at 3.</strong> Discovering that a style reads wrong inside a 24-credit batch is the expensive version of a cheap question. The z-image probe lane exists so the answer costs 3.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Treating the menu lane like a meter lane.</strong> Hailuo's flat-per-clip pricing has an eligibility grid: 1080p exists only at 6 seconds; 10 seconds bills at 768p. "Twelve seconds in full HD" is not a pricier clip — it is not a clip at all, and the order should fail before it bills.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">People-shaped prompts in marketing scenes.</strong> A park scene with joggers is a demographic signal with a compliance conversation attached. The prompt template itself should say unpopulated — same deliverable, 6 credits, no argument.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Long teasers.</strong> Feed hooks bill per second on the turbo lane (27/s at 720p); a 10-second teaser is exactly twice a 5-second one and converts no better. Five seconds, one room, done.</p>
