            <ul style="color:#d1d5db;">
                <li style="margin-bottom:6px;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Rendering before storyboarding.</strong> The 160-credit storyboard is the cheapest note the director will ever get; skip it and the notes arrive as 540-credit re-sequences.</li>
                <li style="margin-bottom:6px;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Keyframes generated as art, not as contracts.</strong> Station 2 frames must be machine-useful: same aspect, same character description block, generated knowing they're literal inputs to Wan's frame mode — where last-frame without first-frame is rejected outright.</li>
                <li style="margin-bottom:6px;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Forgetting the Σ-seconds meter.</strong> Omni multi-shot bills summed shot seconds, so a "15-second sequence" of five shots is 15 seconds of price — but a loose shot list quietly becomes 15-plus. Write shot seconds into the storyboard and hold Station 3 to them.</li>
                <li style="margin-bottom:6px;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Extension as an afterthought.</strong> Veo extend is 360 flat — a third of a two-shot Kling sequence. If a beat needs more room, extend beats re-render: the render changes everything, the extension keeps it.</li>
