            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Pitfalls: Five Ways a Repurposing Month Overbills Itself</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Padding sponsor reads past a thousand bytes.</strong> Turbo voice bills per byte-block, and crossing 1,000 bytes doubles a 10-credit read to 20. Ad scripts are the place producers add words they don't need — and pay double for them.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Clipping on the without-reference rate.</strong> Seedance mini with a reference still bills 3/s; without, it doubles to 6/s. Driving clips from the episode cover is not a stylistic choice — it is an 8%-of-the-month choice (1,296 vs 648 on this many clips).</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Booking episodes that don't land on whole minutes.</strong> Transcripts round up every partial minute; six 30:20 episodes pay 31 minutes each for the sake of twenty seconds. Trim the recording to clean halves before the STT pass, not after.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Re-rendering music to "try another length."</strong> Suno is flat 20 per operation, so a longer intro is a new operation — but a longer <em>reused</em> bed is free. Generate once, extend deliberately, and loop the finished track across all six episodes without metering it.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Skipping transcripts and clipping by ear.</strong> It feels like saving 1,080, but it re-bills the same cost as producer hours hunting audio and loses the SEO show-notes page the transcript gives you for free. The transcript is a multi-product asset, not overhead.</p>
