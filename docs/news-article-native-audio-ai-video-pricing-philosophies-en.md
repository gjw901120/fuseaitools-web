            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Native audio went from party trick to deliverable requirement in about eighteen months, and the pricing response split three ways. One camp treats sound as a metered upgrade. One rolled it into the base rate and told nobody. One refused to itemize it at all inside a flat per-clip price. If you generate video with sound on — footsteps, ambience, product whooshes — your vendor choice is really a choice between these three philosophies, and the difference shows up on every invoice.</p>

            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">We ran the same brief through all three camps: a 10-second 1080p clip with audio enabled, list prices in credits (100 credits = $1). Monthly plans on <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com" style="color:#f472b6;">FuseAITools</a> meter credits at <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Basic 82%, Pro 73%, Ultra 63%</strong>, so every quote below gets both numbers.</p>

            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Camp 1 — The Surcharge: <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/kling/v3-0-video" style="color:#f472b6;">Kling 3.0</a> (and 3.0 Omni)</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Kling's metering splits every render into silent and sound-on scenes at different per-second rates: standard is 20 credits/sec silent, 30 with sound (+50%); pro tier runs 30 → 40 (+33%). The <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/kling/3-0-omni-text-to-video" style="color:#f472b6;">Omni line</a> mirrors it (720p: 20 → 30; 1080p: 30 → 36) — then does something odd at the top: <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">4K is 100 credits/sec with or without sound</strong>. The surcharge evaporates exactly where the clip is most expensive.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Our brief:</strong> 10 sec × 40 (pro with_sound) = <strong style="color:#f472b6;">400 credits list</strong> (~252 on Ultra monthly). The same clip silent would have been 300. You paid 100 credits — 25% of the clip — for the audio track.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">The philosophy has one virtue and one vice. Virtue: if you render a third of your catalog silent (banners, loops with licensed music laid over later), you never pay for audio you mute in post. Vice: audio becomes a negotiation you forget you're in — the toggle's cost scales with duration, so the clips where sound matters most (longer narrative pieces) carry the biggest surcharge.</p>

        <section class="camp-2">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Camp 2 — All-Inclusive: <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/wan/v3-0-video" style="color:#60a5fa;">Wan 3.0</a> and <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/happy-horse/v1-1-text-to-video" style="color:#34d399;">HappyHorse 1.1</a></h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Wan 3.0 bills one rate per resolution — 12 / 24 / 48 credits per second at 480p / 720p / 1080p — and the audio toggle does not move it. Sound on, sound off, same invoice. <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/happy-horse/v1-1-reference-to-video" style="color:#34d399;">HappyHorse 1.1</a> works the same way (36/sec at 720p, 45 at 1080p across its text, image, and reference modes): native audio is simply part of what the model is.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Our brief:</strong> Wan 10 sec × 48 = <strong style="color:#60a5fa;">480 credits list</strong> (~302 Ultra), sound included at zero marginal cost. HappyHorse lands at 450 (~284) for the same job. Both more expensive than Kling's silent tier — and both cheaper than Kling pro-with-sound (400) only at the margin HappyHorse holds.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">The all-inclusive camp changes your iteration math: since audio is free, you leave it on during drafts and get talking-blocking and ambience reviews for nothing. Teams on surcharge camps often draft silent and re-render with sound at the end — a workflow tax the invoice hides.</p>

            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Camp 3 — Flat and Unitemized: <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/veo3/text-to-video" style="color:#818cf8;">Veo</a> and <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/sora/text-to-video" style="color:#60a5fa;">Sora 2</a></h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">The flat-rate camp never tells you what audio costs because it never asks. Veo 3.1 Lite bills <strong style="color:#818cf8;">24 credits per clip</strong> and Fast 48, native audio and all; the full Veo 3 tier is 360 per generation. Sora 2 is the camp's radical: <strong style="color:#60a5fa;">10 credits per generation</strong>, sound included, at its native durations.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Our brief:</strong> the 10-second sound-on clip costs 400–480 credits in the per-second camps and 24 in Veo Lite's world. The honest asterisk: you don't choose seconds on flat-billed models — you accept their fixed render lengths and styles, and paying the per-second price is precisely what buys duration sliders, resolution ladders, and reference controls.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Where the flat camp wins commercially: volume content with a platform-tolerant look. At Ultra monthly (63% of list), a Veo Lite clip is ~15 credits — cheaper than the audio surcharge on any single Kling clip.</p>
