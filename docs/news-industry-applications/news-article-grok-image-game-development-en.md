            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Workflow 1: Concept Art Exploration</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Every game starts with art direction exploration. The team needs to answer: what does this game look like? Is it dark fantasy or bright sci-fi? Realistic or stylized? Hand-painted or clean vector? Traditional concept art exploration takes weeks and tens of thousands of dollars. AI collapses this into a focused day of visual brainstorming.</p>
            <h3 style="color:#f3f4f6;">Workshop: Art Direction Sprint</h3>
            <div style="background:rgba(31,41,55,0.3);padding:16px;border-radius:8px;margin:16px 0;">
                <p style="color:#94a3b8;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">Art direction exploration prompts:</p>
                <p style="color:#94a3b8;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">"Dark fantasy RPG environment. Ancient stone cathedral overtaken by bioluminescent fungi. Gothic architecture with organic growth. Moody volumetric lighting from stained glass windows. Art styles to explore: realistic painterly, stylized hand-painted, cel-shaded, pixel art interpretation."</p>
                <p style="color:#94a3b8;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">Generate the same scene in 8 art styles side by side. The team reviews and votes. Art direction decided in 2 hours instead of 2 weeks of commissioned concept pieces.</p>
                <p style="color:#94a3b8;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">Iteration cycle:</p>
                <p style="color:#94a3b8;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">1) Broad exploration: 50+ style variations across genres</p>
                <p style="color:#94a3b8;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">2) Shortlist: pick 3 directions, generate 20 variations each</p>
                <p style="color:#94a3b8;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">3) Refinement: take winning direction, generate 50 detail variations</p>
                <p style="color:#94a3b8;font-style:italic;">4) Final: art director creates production guidelines from AI-generated reference board</p>
            </div>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">This sprint replaces the traditional 4–6 week concept art phase. The team enters production with a clear, tested art direction — backed by visual evidence, not just a mood board of borrowed references.</p>
        </section>

        <section class="character-design">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Workflow 2: Character Design Iteration</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Character design is the most iterative art discipline in game development. A single protagonist might go through 30–100 design variations before the team lands on the final look. Each traditional revision cycle costs $300–800 and takes 2–5 days. AI enables unlimited character exploration — trying every armor style, weapon combination, color palette, and proportion variation in a single session.</p>
            <h3 style="color:#f3f4f6;">Workshop: Protagonist Design Sprint</h3>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Design a game protagonist through rapid iteration: start with a character brief (role, personality, world setting). Generate 20 silhouette variations, then 20 costume designs for the best silhouettes, then 20 color palettes for the best costumes, then turn/pose sheets for the final design. Total: 80+ variations explored in 1–2 days. The traditional process would take 4–6 weeks and $5,000–15,000 in artist commissions.</p>
            <div style="background:rgba(31,41,55,0.3);padding:16px;border-radius:8px;margin:16px 0;">
                <p style="color:#94a3b8;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">Character design prompt structure:</p>
                <p style="color:#94a3b8;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">"Game character design sheet. [Class/role: cybernetic bounty hunter]. Setting: [neo-Tokyo 2087]. Body type: [athletic, lean]. Key visual elements: [glowing cybernetic arm, holographic visor, worn leather duster]. Art style: [clean line art with flat color, game production concept]. Show: front view, 3/4 view, back view, detail close-ups of key elements."</p>

        <section class="environment-worldbuilding">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Workflow 3: Environment and World-Building Visualization</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Game worlds need environmental storytelling — every location should communicate mood, history, and narrative through visual design. Before 3D artists build environments, the team needs paintings that establish the visual language: lighting mood, architectural style, vegetation, atmosphere, and scale references. Generate comprehensive world-building visual libraries.</p>
            <h3 style="color:#f3f4f6;">Workshop: Game World Visual Bible</h3>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Create a visual bible for a fantasy game world: 5 major regions, each with 10 environment paintings (50 total). Forest kingdom (ancient trees, fairy lights, moss-covered ruins). Desert empire (sandstone palaces, harsh sunlight, oasis gardens). Underground realm (crystal caverns, bioluminescent rivers, dwarf-carved halls). Each region gets consistent visual language — architecture, vegetation, lighting, atmosphere — that guides the 3D art team for months of production.</p>
        </section>

        <section class="ui-icon-assets">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Workflow 4: UI and Icon Asset Generation</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Games need hundreds of UI elements: item icons, ability icons, resource indicators, map markers, inventory slots, and interface decorations. Each icon needs to be readable at small sizes, consistent in style, and thematically appropriate. AI generates complete icon sets that maintain visual consistency across hundreds of items.</p>
            <h3 style="color:#f3f4f6;">Workshop: RPG Item Icon Set</h3>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Generate a 200-icon RPG item set: 40 weapons (swords, bows, staves, daggers, maces), 40 armor pieces (helmets, chestplates, boots, gloves, shields), 40 consumables (potions, scrolls, food, herbs), 40 materials (ore, wood, cloth, gems, hides), and 40 misc items (keys, maps, quest items). All in a consistent art style — clean, readable at 64x64 pixels, with consistent lighting and color palette. Production time: 1–2 days instead of 3–4 weeks of icon artist work.</p>
