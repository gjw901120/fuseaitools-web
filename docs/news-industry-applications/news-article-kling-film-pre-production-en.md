For independent filmmakers and smaller productions, comprehensive pre-viz is often skipped entirely due to cost, leading to expensive on-set decision-making.</p>

The director can test camera angles, lighting moods, pacing choices, and visual approaches before committing to them on set — where changes cost thousands per minute.</p>

        <section class="scene-previz">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Workflow 1: Scene Pre-Visualization Generation</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Every scene in a script needs visualization before shooting. Directors need to see the scene moving — camera positions, actor blocking, lighting mood, pacing — before they step onto set. AI generates cinematic pre-viz videos from scene descriptions, giving directors a moving reference for every sequence.</p>
            <h3 style="color:#f3f4f6;">Workshop: Script-to-PreViz Pipeline</h3>
            <div style="background:rgba(31,41,55,0.3);padding:16px;border-radius:8px;margin:16px 0;">
                <p style="color:#f87171;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">Pre-viz generation from script:</p>
                <p style="color:#f87171;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">1) Break script into individual scenes with shot descriptions</p>
                <p style="color:#f87171;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">2) For each scene, define: location, time of day, lighting mood, camera movement, character positions</p>
                <p style="color:#f87171;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">3) Generate pre-viz video: "Cinematic pre-visualization. [Scene description]. Camera: [handheld tracking shot / steady dolly / static wide]. Lighting: [harsh fluorescent / warm golden hour / cold blue moonlight]. Characters: [positions and movements]. Mood: [tense / romantic / contemplative]. Duration: [scene length]."</p>
                <p style="color:#f87171;font-style:italic;margin-bottom:8px;">4) Review and iterate: try different camera angles, lighting choices, and pacing</p>
                <p style="color:#f87171;font-style:italic;">5) Compile scene pre-viz into sequence reels for crew review</p>
            </div>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">A 100-page script can be pre-visualized in 3–5 days instead of the 8–12 weeks traditional storyboard and animatic production requires. Every scene gets visual exploration, not just the key sequences that fit the budget.</p>
        </section>

        <section class="storyboard-animation">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Workflow 2: Storyboard-to-Video Animation</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Directors who work with storyboard artists can use AI to animate those storyboards into moving pre-viz. Static storyboard panels become animated sequences with camera movement, character motion, and atmospheric effects — bridging the gap between illustration and final footage.</p>
            <h3 style="color:#f3f4f6;">Workshop: Animated Storyboard Reel</h3>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Take a completed storyboard (50 panels for a key action sequence) and animate each panel into a 3–5 second video clip. Compile into a 3-minute animated storyboard reel with camera movements, character motion, and basic sound design. The director can screen this for the crew to communicate exact visual intentions before shooting begins.</p>
        </section>

        <section class="pitch-materials">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Workflow 3: Visual Pitch Material Production</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Films need to be pitched — to studios, investors, talent, and distributors. A compelling visual pitch deck with pre-viz footage dramatically increases the chances of greenlighting. AI generates cinematic pitch materials that show stakeholders exactly what the film will look like.</p>
            <h3 style="color:#f3f4f6;">Workshop: Film Pitch Video Package</h3>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Create a 5-minute pitch video: opening mood sequence (30 seconds establishing visual tone), 5 key scene pre-viz clips (30–60 seconds each showing the most compelling moments), character introduction sequences, and closing montage. The pitch video communicates the director's vision more effectively than any script or deck alone.</p>
        </section>

        <section class="shot-testing">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Workflow 4: Shot Sequence Testing</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Directors need to test different approaches to key scenes — different camera setups, different pacing, different visual tones. AI enables rapid shot sequence testing: generate the same scene 5 different ways and compare before committing on set.</p>
            <h3 style="color:#f3f4f6;">Workshop: Key Scene Shot Testing</h3>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">For a critical dramatic scene, generate 5 different visual approaches: intimate close-up coverage, wide establishing shot, handheld documentary style, composed symmetrical framing, and dynamic tracking shot. Screen all 5 for the director and DP. Choose the approach that serves the story best. On set, the team executes with confidence — the creative decision is already made and tested.</p>
        </section>
