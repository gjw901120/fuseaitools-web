            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Introduction: The Art Budget Is Three Budgets Wearing One Vest</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">A four-person studio prepping a launch quarter asked for "game art." What they actually needed was three separate production lines with three different billing shapes: <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">still images priced per file</strong> (concept, standing art, sprites, key art), <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">video priced per second</strong> (the launch PV), and <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">audio priced per operation and per byte</strong> (theme track and character VO). On <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/flux-kontext" style="color:#818cf8;text-decoration:none;">FuseAI</a>, the whole quarter's art package — concept exploration, six locked characters, 40 sprite cutouts, hero key art, a 15-second multi-shot PV, a theme track, and bilingual VO — lands at <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">1,666 list credits</strong>: 1,366 at Basic's 82%, 1,216 at Pro's 73%, 1,049 at Ultra's 63%. The outsourcing quote for the same spec started at four figures in dollars per <em>character</em>.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">This article walks each station with the live rate card open, because the surprises are structural, not artistic: the PV costs more than every still image combined, the sprite sheet's transparent backgrounds only exist at one resolution, and the cheapest draft lane is a different vendor family than the final. Character consistency is handled as an editing problem, not a prompting prayer.</p>

        <section itemprop="articleSection" style="margin-bottom:40px;">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Stage 1: Concept Exploration — Batch Pricing Is a Dial, Not a Tax</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Concept work is the cheapest stage per decision because <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/flux-kontext/flux-3-klein-text-to-image" style="color:#818cf8;text-decoration:none;">Flux 3 Klein</a> prices per image by resolution tier — 3 credits at 1K, 6 at 2K, 12 at 4K — and the real charge is that unit <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">multiplied by numImages (mandatory, 1–4)</strong>. Read correctly, that multiplier is a feature: exploration wants options. Thirty concept orders at 1K with numImages=4 is <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">120 images for 360 list credits</strong> — three full silhouette boards per character before anything is "chosen." Both <code>resolution</code> and <code>numImages</code> are billing keys, so the form cannot submit without them; the budget cannot drift by omission, only by decision.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">The mistake pattern inverts the dial: teams that lock finals at 4K and draft at 4K too pay 4× for pixels they will throw away. Draft at 1K (3), iterate at 2K (6), ship finals at 4K (12) — the pyramid is the same one the cost-engineering series measured on video, and game concept art is its most natural home because exploration volume is high and discard rate is ~90%.</p>

        <section itemprop="articleSection" style="margin-bottom:40px;">
            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Stage 2: Character Lock-In and Sprites — Consistency Is an Edit, Transparency Is a Resolution</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Lock-in runs on the edit endpoint.</strong> <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/flux-kontext/flux-3-klein-edit-image" style="color:#818cf8;text-decoration:none;">Klein edit-image</a> is priced identically to text-to-image (3/6/12 per image × numImages) and adds <code>auto</code> to the aspect list, so pose variations, expression sets, and outfit swaps on a chosen silhouette are edits of a known file — not fresh generations hoping the seed behaves. Six characters × four iteration orders each × 2K × numImages=2 = <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">288 list credits</strong>, and every one of them starts from the same face.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Sprites collide with the transparency clause.</strong> Engine-ready sprites need alpha, and on this platform the parameter that produces it — <code>background=transparent</code> on <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/gpt-image/v2-5-flare-text-to-image" style="color:#818cf8;text-decoration:none;">GPT Image 2.5 Flare</a> — is a <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">1K-only capability</strong> at the vendor level: request transparent at 2K and the order may pass every gate and still deliver an opaque file that bills 18 credits instead of 6. So the 40-sprite batch runs at 1K transparent: <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">240 list credits</strong>, full alpha, engine-ready. The sprite-to-4K wish is not a pricing failure but a pipeline fact: upscale the locked sprite as its own pass, or let the engine scale — either way, transparency decides the working resolution, not the other way around.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Key art is the one place 4K earns its price.</strong> The store page banner is the only deliverable that renders on a 4K monitor at full width: three Klein 4K finals plus one hero variant, four files at 12 credits each = <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">48 list credits</strong>. Spending more list than that on key art is a taste argument; spending less is a pixel-density argument the storefront will lose.</p>

            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Stage 3: The Launch PV — Draft on the Mini Lane, Final on Omni, and the Σ-Shot Clock</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">The 15-second trailer is the most expensive single line in the package <em>and</em> the cheapest to rehearse, because the draft and final lanes belong to different families. Draft: <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/seedance/v2-mini" style="color:#818cf8;text-decoration:none;">Seedance 2 mini</a> runs 6 credits per second at 480p without a reference video — one full 15-second assembly is <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">90 list credits</strong> to learn that shot two is boring. Final: the locked characters are images, so the PV is an <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/kling/3-0-omni-image-to-video" style="color:#818cf8;text-decoration:none;">Omni image-to-video</a> job — same rate card as text-to-video: 1080p with audio is 36/s, without audio 30/s.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Two billing behaviors decide the PV invoice. First, <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">multi-shot orders bill Σ shot-seconds</strong>: a three-shot trailer of 5-second cuts is charged on 15 total seconds, not on "one video" — 15 × 36 = <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">540 list credits</strong> for the 1080p with-audio final. Second, audio is a scene switch priced per second: if the PV's sound comes from the Suno track (it does), generate the visual with <code>audio=false</code> and the same cut drops to 30/s — <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">180 credits of difference on one checkbox</strong>. The trailer's native SFX lane (whooshes the model invents) is exactly what the edit replaces; paying 6/s for sound you will mute is the most avoidable line on the sheet.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;">Music and VO close the package. <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/suno/generate" style="color:#818cf8;text-decoration:none;">Suno</a> bills a flat 20 per operation: one generate plus two extends to reach a 3-minute loop = 60. <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/elevenlabs/turbo-2-5" style="color:#818cf8;text-decoration:none;">ElevenLabs turbo</a> bills UTF-8 byte-thousandths at 10: the 900-byte English launcher VO is 10, and its Chinese sibling — same words, ~3 bytes per glyph — lands at 3 blocks = 30. Bilingual is not double; for CJK it is <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">triple</strong>, and the byte is why.</p>

            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Pitfalls: Five Ways the Art Budget Leaks Anyway</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Drafting at 4K.</strong> The resolution tier is the price tier: 12 credits of final-quality pixels per throwaway concept is 4× the draft budget on pixels with a 90% discard rate. The pyramid — 1K explore, 2K iterate, 4K ship — is the whole cost story of Stage 1.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Asking a sprite for transparency at 2K.</strong> The 1K-only clause on <code>background=transparent</code> has no enforcement popup; a 2K request can deliver a billed, opaque, useless file. Transparency picks the resolution. Plan the upscale separately or let the engine do it.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Re-generation instead of editing for consistency.</strong> A fresh text-to-image roll for "the same character, new pose" restarts the identity lottery. Klein edit is the same price per image; use the file you already approved as the input, and the sixth character variation finally has the same face as the first.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Counting the PV as "one video" instead of Σ seconds.</strong> Three 5-second shots are 15 billable seconds at the scene rate — 540 with audio, 450 without. Know which clock the order runs on before the storyboard expands from 2 shots to 4.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Pricing bilingual VO as 2×.</strong> ElevenLabs bills UTF-8 bytes; a Chinese script triples the blocks of its English twin. Budget the CJK lane in bytes, not in sentence counts — the 10-becomes-30 line in the invoice is structural.</p>
        </section>

            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Closing: The Art Director Is Now Also the Invoice</h2>
