Twelve credits a second by back-division, but you could not buy a cheaper resolution or a silent variant, because the menu predates both ideas. Audio was exactly double, on every duration, in both t2v and i2v. This is the moment the bill stopped being a function of your edit list and started being a function of your asset plan — and every later jump is a retreat from the premium 2.6 established.</p>

Two dimensions, clip-length freedom, no rounding to menu durations. The future, at a price designed to introduce it.</p>

Three collapses in one matrix: the audio surcharge fell from 2.6's exact 2× to 1.5× at 720p and 1.2× at 1080p and zero at 4K; the silent rate undercut even V3 Turbo's no-scene 27; and the scene key was renamed to <code>no_audio/with_audio</code> — a different spelling from 3.0-video's <code>without_sound/with_sound</code>, so two generations of cost sheets do not port. Default duration: 5 seconds; multi-shot orders bill <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Σ shot-seconds</strong> (jump 8), not per clip — a three-cut teaser priced as its total runtime.</p>

Finer units did not raise the price; they moved the discount to the lane you can verify before you render.</p>

Your inputs, not just your outputs, are now on the invoice: the same 10 seconds costs 200 silent, 300 voiced, or 450 if you steer it with a reference clip at 1080p.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Jump 10: duration stopped being a choice.</strong> <a href="https://www.fuseaitools.com/home/kling/3-0-omni-transformation" style="color:#2dd4bf;text-decoration:none;">Omni transformation</a> (video-to-video, up to four reference images) bills <code>duration × quality</code> at <strong style="color:#f9fafb;">30/45/100 per second</strong> — but the duration is <em>your uploaded source video's actual length</em>, probed and rounded up to whole seconds within 3–15. There is no scene dimension because a video-input render never generates audio; there is no duration slider because the file decides. It is the terminal shape of this decade-long trend: every knob the flat menu offered became either a priced selector or a measured fact.</p>

            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Pitfalls: Five Ways Old Kling Habits Misprice New Kling</h2>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Budgeting the 9/s motion-control era.</strong> The introduction rate is gone — October 15 doubled it to 18/27 in a single deploy. Any sheet citing the old number is understating by half, and the changelog says so out loud.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Porting scene keys across generations.</strong> 3.0 video says <code>without_sound/with_sound</code>; Omni says <code>no_audio/with_audio</code>. Same concept, different spelling, different lookup — automation that copies a cost model between the two fails on the join, not on the price.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Paying the 2× audio premium out of habit.</strong> 2.6 priced sound at exactly double; Omni charges +10 at 720p-per-second and +6 at 1080p, and nothing at 4K. Teams that "always budget audio ×2" now overplan by up to 100 credits per second at the top tier, where the surcharge no longer exists.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Quoting transformation by your timeline.</strong> Duration is the uploaded source file's real seconds, rounded up — not a slider. A 12.2-second source bills 13; a "we'll trim it in post" plan is a billing decision, and the trim should happen before the upload.</p>
            <p style="color:#d1d5db;"><strong style="color:#f9fafb;">Billing multi-shot as multiple clips.</strong> Omni's multi-shot orders charge Σ shot-seconds inside the 3–15 window. Three 4-second cuts are 12 seconds of price, not three minimums — storyboards should count total runtime, and the 5-second default duration is a fallback, not a plan.</p>

            <h2 style="color:#f9fafb;border-bottom-color:#374151;">Closing: The Unit Always Wins</h2>
